# Playing games on your TRMNL

> Source for the help-center article. Player-facing — no code.

Your TRMNL can host a game. Chess, Battleship, and whatever else people build.
Two people scan a code off the screen, take a seat, and play from their phones
while the screen shows the board to the whole room.

## Read this first, or the rest will seem broken

**Your phone is where you play. The screen is where everyone watches.**

A move you make shows up on your phone instantly. It shows up on the TRMNL up to
15–20 minutes later, on its next refresh. That is not a bug and it is not
something to wait for — e-ink screens refresh on a budget, which is exactly why
they run for months on a battery.

So think of it like a board on the wall rather than a screen you play on. It
tells the room a game is happening and whose turn it is. The playing happens in
your hand.

Games that suit this are the unhurried kind — a chess game over a week, a round
of Battleship across an afternoon. Anything needing fast back-and-forth will
feel wrong.

## Getting a game on your screen

1. Find the game in **Recipes** — search for *Arcade Chess* or *Arcade
   Battleship*.
2. Install it.
3. Add it to a playlist and wait for the next refresh.

That's the whole setup. There's no game code to enter and nothing to configure —
the game belongs to the device it's installed on.

*(During the beta the recipes are unlisted, so use the direct link you were sent
rather than searching.)*

## Taking a seat

When the game appears, the screen says **SCAN TO PLAY** with a QR code for each
open seat.

1. Scan one with your phone.
2. You'll land on your player page. **Bookmark it.**
3. Have the other player scan the other code.

Once both seats are taken, the game starts and the board replaces the lobby on
the next refresh.

### Really do bookmark it

That page's address is your seat. There is no login and no way to recover it —
if you close the tab and lose the address, you cannot get back into that game.
That sounds harsh, and it's deliberate: any "recover my seat" link on the shared
screen could be used by anyone who can see the screen, which in Battleship would
hand a passer-by your fleet.

Bookmark it, or leave the tab open. If you do lose it, finish the game on the
honour system or start a new one.

## Making moves

Your player page shows the board, whose turn it is, and a box to type your move.
Chess takes standard notation (`e4`, `Nf3`, `O-O`). Battleship takes a square
(`B7`).

If the move isn't legal you'll be told, and nothing changes. If it's your
opponent's turn you'll be told that too. Then wait — for them to move, and for
the screen to catch up.

## Hidden information

Some games have secrets. In Battleship your fleet is yours alone: your phone
shows your ships, the shared screen shows only hits and misses, and your
opponent's page never shows your ships anywhere.

This is why the seat page is a bookmark and not a link on the screen — the whole
guarantee rests on your address being yours.

## Starting another game

When a game finishes, the screen shows the result and a **PLAY AGAIN** code.
Scan it to clear the board and reopen both seats.

It won't work mid-game — that's on purpose, so nobody wipes a live board by
scanning a code they found interesting.

## Things worth knowing

- **Anyone who can see the screen can take an open seat.** For a game on your
  kitchen wall that's the point. For an office, expect a colleague to join.
- **A game nobody finishes stays put for a week,** then clears itself.
- **One game per screen per type.** Chess and Battleship can run side by side,
  but two chess games on one TRMNL can't.
- **Moving the recipe to another device starts a fresh game.** The old one clears
  on its own.

## If something looks wrong

- **Screen still shows the lobby after someone scanned** — the seat is claimed;
  the screen just hasn't refreshed. Check your phone.
- **"Seat already taken"** — someone got there first. Take the other seat.
- **"Not your seat"** — that address belongs to a game that has since been reset.
  Scan a fresh code.
- **Nothing on screen at all** — make sure the plugin is on a playlist and has
  had one refresh cycle to run.
